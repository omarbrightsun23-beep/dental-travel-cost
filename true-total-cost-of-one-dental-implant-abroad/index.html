The True Total Cost of One Dental Implant Abroad: Finished Tooth + Both Trips

By the DentalTravelCost team · Prices checked November 2026

Quick answer: A headline price such as "$300 for a dental implant" abroad usually buys only part of the treatment — on the page we checked, the screw alone, without the abutment or the crown that make it a tooth you can chew with. A finished single implant in Turkey — fixture, placement, abutment and crown — is priced at €500–1,800 in the Acıbadem International price index for Q3 2026, and a finished premium-brand tooth is documented at €804–982 by BestDoctorTurkey. Then add travel twice: implants take two trips, 3–6 months apart, so flights, hotel nights, food and transfers are paid two times, not once. The worked example in this article puts one finished implant in Turkey, travel included, at about €2,300 in total on clearly labelled mid-range assumptions — still below the UK and US treatment-only ranges quoted below, but a very different number from any screw-only headline.

Disclaimer: this article is general information about treatment costs and timelines, not medical advice. Whether an implant is suitable for you, which parts and brand your case needs, and how long healing takes in your case, can only be decided by a dentist who has examined you and seen your scans.

The headline-price trap: a screw is not a tooth

Open almost any clinic price page and the first implant number you see is the smallest one the clinic can honestly print. The problem is not always that the number is false. The problem is what it leaves out.

Take the competitor page this article set out to check. DentaVacation's dental implants in Turkey page — verified live on 26 November 2026 — leads with a "$300 single implant" headline. Read further down that same page and its own table gives a different figure for the complete job: $510–1,500 for a single implant with abutment and crown. In other words, the headline and the finished tooth are not the same product. The $300 buys the implant fixture. The part that connects it to your mouth, and the crown you actually bite with, sit in the higher range underneath.

BestDoctorTurkey documents the same pattern in euros, and puts a proportion on it. An implant advertised at €179, it notes, is the screw alone — about 18% of the price of the finished tooth. The other 82% or so is everything that turns a titanium screw in the jaw into a working tooth: placing it, connecting it, and crowning it.

Neither example means the higher figure is unfair — surgery, components and laboratory work cost money. It means you cannot compare a screw-only headline with a finished-tooth quote from another clinic, or with a UK or US price that already includes the crown. Match the scope first. Only then compare the numbers.

What a finished tooth actually consists of

One "dental implant", as patients use the phrase, is four things bought together:

Part or stageWhat it isIn a screw-only headline?
Implant fixtureThe titanium screw placed in the jawboneYes — this is usually the headline item
Placement (surgery)The operation to put the fixture in, with scans, anaesthetic and follow-up checksOften not — see the tariff note below
AbutmentThe connector fitted on top of the healed fixtureNo
CrownThe visible, porcelain tooth fitted onto the abutmentNo

The placement line deserves its own warning, because it can be missing even when the fixture itself is included. The Turkish Dentists' Association (TDB) 2026 tariff lists implant placement at €401 — explicitly excluding the implant itself. So a quote can contain a fixture price with no surgery fee, a surgery fee with no fixture, or both together, and all three quotes can be described in advertising as an "implant price". Unless the written quote separates these lines, you do not know which version you are holding.

What does the complete set cost when it is priced as one job? Two independent sources checked in November 2026 give consistent ranges:

  • BestDoctorTurkey: a finished premium-brand tooth — Straumann or Nobel — at €804–982.
  • Acıbadem International price index (Q3 2026): a single implant plus crown in Turkey at €500–1,800.

The spread inside those ranges is real, and is mostly explained by brand, crown material, and whether extra work such as a bone graft or sinus lift is needed. A standard-brand tooth can sit near the bottom of the Acıbadem range; a premium system, with grafting, moves towards the top. That is why the worked example below uses a labelled mid-range assumption — your written, itemised quote replaces it.

The second cost the headline hides: you travel twice

Even a perfectly honest finished-tooth quote is still only the treatment. Getting to it costs money twice, because an implant cannot be finished in one visit.

The fixture placed on the first trip has to fuse with the jawbone before it can safely carry a final crown. That healing period — osseointegration — is 3–6 months, as set out in our guide to how many trips dental implants abroad take. Trip 1 is surgery and temporary teeth. Trip 2, months later, is the healing check, the abutment and the final crown. DentaVacation's own page reflects the same shape: it plans treatment over 2 trips, with trip lengths in its snapshot of 8–10 days for the first trip and 7 days for the second. Other clinics plan shorter stays for a single implant, so treat those lengths as that page's plan, not a universal standard — but the two-trip structure itself is not optional, and no clinic can honestly remove it.

That structure changes the travel maths in three ways:

  • Two return flights, not one — bought months apart, at whatever prices apply then.
  • Two blocks of hotel nights, food and transfers, not one.
  • Two blocks of time off work, which is a real cost even though no invoice records it.

A comparison that sets a Turkish treatment price against a UK price and stops there has quietly deleted the second block. For a full arch, the treatment saving is usually large enough to absorb two trips comfortably. For a single tooth, the margin is thinner, and travel can decide whether the trip is worth it at all.   • Treatment, €900: an illustrative mid-range figure for one finished tooth, chosen inside the sourced ranges above — within Acıbadem's €500–1,800 for an implant plus crown in Turkey, and within BestDoctorTurkey's €804–982 for a finished premium-brand tooth.   • Scope: the €900 is assumed to cover the fixture, its placement, the abutment and the final crown at matched scope. It does not include bone grafting, a sinus lift, extractions, sedation, or treatment for a failed implant — ask whether your case needs any of these before relying on the total.
  • Trip lengths, 5 nights each: a planning assumption for a single implant, used so the example stays readable. Clinics plan different lengths — DentaVacation's snapshot, for example, is longer — and your clinic's written plan for your case governs. More nights raise the hotel and food lines directly.
  • Flights, €250 return per trip; hotel, €60 per night; food and transfers, €150 per trip: illustrative example budget lines only. They are not researched fares, not averages and not live prices. Replace each one with an actual quote for your route, dates and standard of hotel before making any decision.
  • Not included: time off work, travel insurance, a companion's costs, and any third trip if an implant fails to integrate. None of these is free; they are excluded only because they vary too much between readers to price honestly in one table.

Two lessons fall out of the arithmetic. First, travel is €1,400 of the €2,300 total — about 61% of it. For a single tooth, the journey costs more than the dentistry on these mid-range assumptions, which is the opposite of what a treatment-only headline suggests. Second, the comparison with home is still favourable, but it is a comparison of totals: the Acıbadem International price index (Q3 2026) puts a single implant plus crown at £1,800–3,500 in the UK and $3,000–6,000 in the US, treatment only, with no travel needed. Set €2,300 all-in against those ranges and travelling can still save money — just not the five-figure saving, and not the "$300 tooth", that a screw-only headline implies. The saving also grows quickly if you genuinely need more than one tooth treated, because the same two trips carry all of it.

How to read any clinic quote: a short checklist

Use these questions on every quote, from every clinic, before you compare prices or pay a deposit. A clinic that answers them in writing is giving you a price you can use. A clinic that will not is giving you a headline.

  • Is this price for the fixture only, or the finished tooth? Ask for the words "including abutment and crown" in writing.
  • Is placement included? The TDB 2026 tariff prices placement at €401, excluding the implant itself — so ask whether surgery, scans and follow-up checks are inside your figure or added to it.
  • Which implant brand and crown material am I paying for? Premium systems such as Straumann and Nobel price differently from standard brands, and the brand belongs on the quote — and later, in your treatment records.
  • What is excluded? Bone graft, sinus lift, extractions, temporary teeth, sedation and medication are the usual extras. Get each one priced, or confirmed as not needed, before you fly.
  • How many trips, and how many days each? Get both trip lengths in the written plan, and ask what is paid on Trip 1 and what is paid on Trip 2.
  • What happens if the implant does not integrate? Who pays for the replacement treatment — and do you pay your own travel for an extra trip? Read our guide to what happens when dental work abroad goes wrong before you need the answer.
  • Can I see the full itemised total before I book flights? One page, every line, one final figure. That figure — plus your two trips of travel — is the only price worth comparing.

FAQ

How much does a dental implant really cost abroad?
For one finished tooth in Turkey — fixture, placement, abutment and crown — the sourced ranges checked in November 2026 are €500–1,800 (Acıbadem International price index, Q3 2026) and €804–982 for a finished premium-brand tooth (BestDoctorTurkey). Add two trips of travel on top: in this article's worked example, €1,400 of illustrative travel brought the true total to about €2,300.

Why do clinics advertise implants for $300 or €179?
Because those figures price the fixture — the screw — on its own. DentaVacation's "$300 single implant" headline sits above its own table pricing the same treatment with abutment and crown at $510–1,500, and BestDoctorTurkey documents a €179 advertised implant as the screw alone, about 18% of the finished tooth. The finished tooth is the figure to compare.

How many trips does one implant take, and how long is the gap?
Two trips. Trip 1 places the implant; after a healing gap of 3–6 months at home, Trip 2 fits the abutment and final crown. Our two-trip timeline guide walks through each trip day by day.

Is one implant abroad still cheaper than at home?
On the figures checked here, usually yes, but by less than the headlines suggest. Treatment plus both trips came to about €2,300 in the worked example, against treatment-only ranges of £1,800–3,500 in the UK and $3,000–6,000 in the US (Acıbadem International, Q3 2026). The saving is smallest for a single tooth, because two trips of travel are fixed costs however small the treatment is — and it disappears if extra work or a third trip is needed, so the itemised quote matters.

What should a proper implant quote include?
The fixture, its placement, the abutment and the crown, each named; the implant brand and crown material; any grafting, extractions or temporaries your case needs; both trip lengths; the payment split between trips; and the clinic's terms if an implant fails to integrate. If any line is missing, ask for it in writing before you book.
