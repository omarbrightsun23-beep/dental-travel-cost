Crossing the Border for Dental Work: San Ysidro (Tijuana) and Andrade (Los Algodones) — How the Days Actually Run

By the DentalTravelCost team · Prices checked November 2026

Mexico is the drive-to corridor: no long-haul flight, no jet lag — the border crossing is the journey, and it runs on the crossing's clock, not yours. The two crossings US dental patients actually use work very differently. San Ysidro, for Tijuana, is a 24-hour city crossing. Andrade, for Los Algodones, is a small-town gate that locks at 10 p.m. This guide covers what to carry, how a clinic day is timed at each crossing, and when a same-day return is sensible — and when it is not. All-in costs for the corridor are in our calculator; where to sleep on both sides is in our Mexico hotel guide.

What to carry (documents and paperwork)

As general guidance — confirm the current rules on the official CBP site before you travel, because entry requirements do change:

  • A valid US passport or passport card. US citizens need a WHTI-compliant document to re-enter the United States at a land crossing; a passport card is made for exactly this and fits in a wallet.
  • Your clinic's appointment confirmation — name, address, date and time. If an officer asks the purpose of your trip, a printed booking answers it in one line.
  • Prescription paperwork for any medication you are carrying back, kept in its original pharmacy packaging.
  • A payment plan: confirm with your clinic beforehand which cards or cash they take, so a large bill never depends on a card working at the desk.
  • Your return plan written down: which crossing, its closing time (Andrade), and the live CBP wait-time page bookmarked on your phone.

San Ysidro → Tijuana: the 24-hour city crossing

Tijuana's dental district is Zona Río — the business and medical quarter along Paseo de los Héroes and Blvd. Sánchez Taboada, about 3 miles (5 km) from the San Ysidro crossing, and not the old Centro/Revolución tourist strip. The working pattern most patients use:

  • Cross on foot at PedEast, the main pedestrian crossing, open 24 hours in both directions. PedWest exists but its hours are limited and have changed repeatedly (most recently 6 a.m.–2 p.m. northbound) — default to PedEast and check live CBP border wait times before your return.
  • Don't drive across unless you must. Vehicle queues to re-enter the US are routinely far longer than the pedestrian line. Stay in Zona Río and use taxis or rideshares between the hotel, the clinic and the border.
  • How the days run: implant and All-on-4 work follows the same rhythm as anywhere — most Tijuana itineraries run 5–7 nights, with surgery early in the trip, check-ups in between, and the final check before you head home. The 24-hour crossing means your departure day is flexible; the recovery is not. Stay at least 48–72 hours after surgery before a long flight, and apply the same rest logic to a long drive home.
  • Same-day trips are for consultations, scans, impressions and minor work — cross in the morning, be treated, walk back the same day.

Andrade → Los Algodones: the 10 p.m. town

Los Algodones (officially Vicente Guerrero), 7 miles west of Yuma, Arizona, packs 350+ dental clinics and dozens of pharmacies into a few walkable blocks — the whole tourist zone is about four blocks. Everything about a trip here is shaped by one fact: the Andrade Port of Entry opens 6:00 a.m.–10:00 p.m. Pacific Time daily, and there is no overnight crossing. The town itself runs on Arizona time, which catches people out — set your watch to the gate, and always check live CBP status before travelling.

  • Park on the US side and walk in: the standard pattern is the Quechan Tribe parking lot on the US side (about $5–$10/day), then a walk of about 2 minutes across the line. Clinics, pharmacies and hotels are a few blocks from the gate.
  • How the days run: cross early, because the town works to the gate's clock. Morning appointments, lab work and pharmacy runs all happen inside one compact zone; a crowns trip here often runs just 2–4 days, because the local labs work fast and nothing is far away.
  • If you sleep on the US side (Yuma hotels are 15–20 minutes' drive from the crossing), your last appointment of the day must leave you time to walk back before the 10 p.m. close — miss it and you are sleeping in Mexico whether you planned to or not.
  • If you sleep in town, the clock disappears: the in-town hotels in our Mexico hotel guide run $47–$100 a night and sit inside the same few blocks as the clinics.

Same-day return: when it works, when it doesn't

Trip type Same-day return?
Consultation, scans, treatment plan Yes — the classic day trip at both crossings
Impressions, minor work, crown fitting checks Usually yes, if the clinic schedules you early
Crowns in Los Algodones Often a short 2–4 day trip rather than one day — the lab needs its days
Implant surgery / All-on-4 placement No — stay the recommended nights (5–7 in Tijuana); the border line is not a recovery room

All-on-4 is two trips wherever you have it: placement first, the final bridge after 3–6 months of healing. Timing checklist for crossing days

  • Check live CBP border wait times on the morning of every return crossing — waits, not distance, set your departure time.
  • At San Ysidro, default to PedEast (24h); treat PedWest hours as unconfirmed until you check them.
  • At Andrade, plan backwards from the 10 p.m. Pacific close, and remember the town runs on Arizona time.
  • After surgery, build in a buffer day before any long drive or flight — 48–72 hours of rest first, as on every corridor.
  • Tell your clinic which side of the border you are sleeping on; good clinics schedule around the gate.

Border-crossing FAQ

What documents do I need to cross the border for dental work? As general guidance: US citizens re-enter the US on a valid US passport or passport card, and you should carry your clinic's appointment confirmation and any prescription paperwork for medication you are bringing back. Entry rules and acceptable documents can change — check the official CBP guidance for your crossing before you travel.

Which San Ysidro crossing should I use for Tijuana? PedEast, the main pedestrian crossing, which is open 24 hours in both directions. PedWest hours are limited and have changed repeatedly, so default to PedEast and check live CBP border wait times before your return trip.

When does the Andrade crossing at Los Algodones close? The Andrade Port of Entry is open 6:00 a.m.–10:00 p.m. Pacific Time daily, and there is no overnight crossing — if you are sleeping on the US side, you must be back before 10 p.m. Los Algodones itself runs on Arizona time. Always check live CBP status before travelling.

Should I drive across or walk? Walk. At Los Algodones you park on the US side (about $5–$10/day) and walk across in about 2 minutes; at Tijuana the pattern is the same — stay in Zona Río and use taxis or rideshares, because vehicle waits to re-enter the US are routinely far longer than the pedestrian line.

Can I cross back the same day as implant surgery? Same-day return is fine for consultations and minor work, and thousands of patients do it. After implant surgery, no — stay the recommended nights first (most Tijuana itineraries run 5–7 nights). The border line is not a recovery room.
