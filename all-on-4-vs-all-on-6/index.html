All-on-4 vs All-on-6: Which Is Worth the Extra Money?

By the DentalTravelCost team · Prices checked November 2026

Quick answer: All-on-6 typically costs about €500–€1,700 more per arch than All-on-4 in Turkey (prices checked November 2026; the gap depends heavily on clinic and implant brand). The extra money buys two more implants spreading the chewing load — worth it if you have enough bone in the back of the jaw, you grind your teeth or have a strong bite, or you are younger and need the bridge to last decades. If your back-jaw bone is thin, All-on-4 is not the cheap option, it is the designed-for-you option: its angled rear implants exist precisely to work with less bone and often avoid grafting. Bone first, budget second — the decision rule is at the end of this article.

Both treatments replace a full jaw of teeth with a fixed bridge you do not take out. Neither is "better" in the abstract, and no honest clinic should sell you the six-implant version as a simple upgrade. Here is what actually differs, what the extra implants cost at real clinics, and how to decide.

Disclaimer: this article is general information to help you compare options and costs, not medical advice. Whether All-on-4, All-on-6 or another treatment suits you can only be decided by a dentist who has examined you and seen your 3D scan.

What the two treatments actually are

All-on-4 uses four implants per jaw: two placed straight at the front, where bone is usually densest, and two at the back tilted at an angle (up to about 45 degrees). The tilt is the point of the design — it lets the rear implants anchor in available bone, reach further back to support the bridge, and in many cases avoid a sinus lift or bone graft in the upper jaw.

All-on-6 uses six implants per jaw, spread more evenly along the arch, with the extra two placed in the back-jaw region. That requires enough bone volume back there to place them — or grafting first to build it.

Everything else is broadly the same: the same two-trip pattern (surgery and temporary bridge first, final bridge after 3–6 months of healing), the same choice of bridge materials, and the same dependence on the surgeon's planning. The differences that matter to you are load, bone, failure behaviour and price.

A note on that failure row, because it is the difference patients feel most. With four implants, each one is structural: lose one and the bridge it carries is compromised. With six, there is redundancy. That does not make All-on-4 fragile — it has a long clinical track record — but it does mean the cost of a single failure is higher, which matters more the longer the bridge needs to last.

There is also a hidden-cost asymmetry. If All-on-6 needs a graft or sinus lift to make the rear implants possible, the graft is an extra fee and extra healing time before the final bridge — so the true gap in your case can be wider than the headline gap. Conversely, if a clinic quotes All-on-4 and then adds grafting anyway, ask why the angled design is not doing the job it exists to do.

When six implants earn their money — and when they do not

The clinical logic, in plain terms:

The case for All-on-6. More implants means each implant carries less load. A 2026 literature review comparing the two protocols found comparable integration and survival for both, with a possible advantage for All-on-6 in specific higher-risk situations — teeth grinding (bruxism), low bone density, and long bridge cantilevers (the section of bridge extending past the last implant) — while stressing the finding is not consistent across all studies. Clinics' own guidance points the same way: six implants are commonly recommended for the upper jaw (where bone is often softer), for patients with strong bite forces or grinding, and for younger patients, simply because a bridge placed at 45 has to survive far more chewing years than one placed at 75, and the redundancy of six is worth more over that horizon.

The case for All-on-4. If the back of your jaw has lost bone — very common in the upper jaw after years without teeth — the angled All-on-4 design was created for exactly that anatomy. It can give you a fixed bridge without grafting: no extra surgery, no extra months of healing, no graft fee. A literature review likewise notes All-on-4 is particularly suited to cases of severe bone loss at the back of the jaw. Choosing All-on-6 in that situation does not buy a better result; it buys a graft you may not need. All-on-4 is also the rational choice where budget is the binding constraint and the bone and bite picture is favourable — the money not spent on two implants is better kept for a zirconia bridge upgrade or a contingency fund.

What does not decide it: the idea that six must be better because six is more. If the scan shows four well-placed implants in good bone with a favourable bite, two more implants add cost and surgery without a matching benefit. And the reverse pressure exists too: a clinic earns more from All-on-6, so if you are steered to six, the reason should be visible on your scan — bone measurements, bite, grinding — not just a brochure.

A worked example: both arches, the real difference

A patient needs both arches, has adequate bone, grinds at night. Using the Istanbul starting prices from the table above (Santé, checked November 2026):

  • All-on-4, both arches: 2 × €2,400 = €4,800
  • All-on-6, both arches: 2 × €3,600 = €7,200
  • Difference: €2,400 for the whole mouth — €1,200 per arch.

Against UK guide ranges for the same work (roughly £10,000–£18,000 per arch for All-on-4 and £12,000–£20,000 for All-on-6), the Turkey gap of €2,400 is small compared with the saving either option represents — which cuts both ways. It means that if your bite and bone genuinely favour six, the upgrade is relatively cheap in Turkey specifically. It also means that if your anatomy favours four, there is no financial pressure to force six: you keep €2,400 and lose nothing your case needed. Note these are starting prices at one clinic before grafts, extractions and bridge-material upgrades; your written quote after a scan is the only number that counts.

FAQ

Is All-on-6 better than All-on-4?
Not automatically. Both have comparable implant survival in the literature. All-on-6 spreads load across more implants and offers redundancy if one fails, which helps in higher-risk cases (grinding, strong bite, softer upper-jaw bone, younger patients). All-on-4 is designed for jaws with bone loss at the back and often avoids grafting. The right choice follows your scan, not the implant count.

What is the cost difference between All-on-4 and All-on-6 in Turkey?
At clinics publishing both prices (checked November 2026), typically about €500–€1,700 more per arch for All-on-6 — from as little as about €190 at one Istanbul clinic's list to about €1,950 at the top of one hospital group's guide range. Brand and bridge material move the gap more than the two extra implants do, so compare like-for-like quotes.

Can I upgrade from All-on-4 to All-on-6 later?
Not as a simple add-on. Adding implants later means new surgery, and the existing bridge usually has to be remade to fit the new layout. That is one reason the decision matters at the start — and why younger patients, with decades of use ahead, are more often advised toward six when their bone allows it.

Does All-on-6 always need a bone graft?
No. It needs enough bone where the rear implants go. If that bone is present, no graft is needed. If it is not, the choice is grafting to make six possible, or All-on-4's angled placement to work with the bone you have. Ask for the bone measurements from your scan either way.

Which lasts longer, All-on-4 or All-on-6?
There is no verified headline figure that settles this, and any clinic quoting you a precise lifespan difference is guessing. Survival rates in comparative studies are comparable; All-on-6 may have an edge in higher-risk situations, and its real longevity advantage is practical — if one implant fails, five can often support the bridge while it is fixed, whereas a failed implant under an All-on-4 usually means remaking the bridge around a replacement.

Why do some clinics push All-on-6 for everyone?
Because it is a higher-priced treatment, and because "more implants" is an easy sell. It is sometimes the right recommendation — but it should be justified by your bone, your bite and your age, in that order. If the justification is generic, get a second assessment before you commit.
