How Many Trips for Dental Implants in Turkey? The Full Two-Trip Timeline

By the DentalTravelCost team · Trip lengths and healing periods checked November 2026

Quick answer: Dental implants abroad take two trips. Trip 1 is for surgery — typically 3–7 days for a single implant, 5–7 days for a full arch — when the implants are placed and you leave with temporary teeth. Then you heal at home for 3–6 months while the bone fuses to the implants. Trip 2, typically 3–7 days, is when the final crowns or bridge are fitted. Veneers and crowns are different: one trip of 5–7 days. Anyone who offers you final implant teeth in a single trip is skipping the healing stage, not beating it.

This guide walks through each stage day by day, counts the real days off work, and prices each trip separately — because the second trip is the cost most clinic quotes leave in the small print.

Disclaimer: this article is general information about treatment timelines and costs, not medical advice. How many trips you need, and how long healing takes in your case, can only be decided by a dentist who has examined you and seen your scans.

Why implants cannot be done in one trip

An implant is a titanium post placed in the jawbone. It only works once the bone has grown onto its surface and locked it in place — a process called osseointegration. Across the published clinic guides we checked in November 2026, that healing period is consistently given as 3–6 months: about 3 months is the figure most clinics quote for a straightforward lower-jaw case or an All-on-4, and up to 6 months where bone is softer (the upper jaw), where a bone graft or sinus lift was added, or where the surgeon simply wants more certainty before loading the implant.

That biology sets the whole shape of the treatment:

  • The final crown or bridge must not take full chewing load until integration is confirmed.
  • What you wear in between is a temporary — a temporary crown, a removable denture, or, for full-arch cases, a fixed temporary bridge screwed onto the new implants. Temporaries look like teeth and let you eat soft food; they are not the finished work.
  • "Same-day teeth" and "immediate loading" offers do not change this. They mean you leave Trip 1 with fixed temporary teeth instead of a removable denture. The final teeth still wait for Trip 2.

Trip 1: surgery and temporary teeth

How long Trip 1 takes depends on how much surgery is involved:

TreatmentTrip 1 lengthWhat happens
Single implant, no graft2–3 days at the shortest; many clinics plan 3–7Examination, 3D scan, implant placement, temporary where needed
Several implants3–7 daysStaged surgery, temporaries, post-op check before flying
All-on-4 / All-on-6 (per arch)5–7 daysExtractions if needed, implant placement, fixed temporary bridge fitted
Implants + bone graft / sinus lift5–7 days, sometimes longerGrafting adds healing checks and can lengthen the gap before Trip 2

A typical full-arch Trip 1 runs like this:

  • Day 1: arrival, examination, 3D CBCT scan, final written plan and price confirmed.
  • Day 2: surgery — extractions if planned, implants placed. Most patients rest at the hotel afterwards.
  • Day 3: rest day. Swelling and discomfort usually peak around days 2–3.
  • Days 4–5: temporary bridge or teeth fitted and adjusted; bite checked.
  • Days 6–7: final check, medication and aftercare instructions, flight home.

Build in a spare day. Clinics' own guides put Trip 1 anywhere from 2–3 days (a single implant at one Antalya clinic) to 7–10 days (a full-mouth restoration at another), so the written plan for your case — not a website's headline — is what to book flights around. The healing gap: 3–6 months at home

This is the part of the timeline nobody can shorten for you, and the part to plan your life around:

  • You are at home, working normally, for almost all of it. Expect some soft-food days in the first 1–2 weeks after surgery, then a normal routine.
  • The temporary teeth need care: soft diet at first, no biting hard food on a temporary bridge, and the hygiene routine the clinic gives you.
  • If a graft or sinus lift was done, the surgeon may set the gap nearer 6 months. Smokers and patients with conditions that slow healing are also commonly given the longer interval.
  • Keep the follow-up the clinic asks for — photos, a local X-ray if requested — because Trip 2 dates are confirmed off the healing check, not the calendar alone.

Trip 2: the final teeth

Trip 2 is shorter and involves no surgery in a straightforward case:

TreatmentTrip 2 lengthWhat happens
Single implant crown2–5 daysHealing check, scan/impression, crown fitted
Several implants3–7 daysCrowns or bridge fitted, bite adjusted
All-on-4 / All-on-6 final bridge5–7 days (some clinics plan 7–10)Temporary bridge removed, final bridge tried in, adjusted and fitted
  • Day 1: healing check and scan; the clinic confirms the implants have integrated.
  • Days 2–3: the lab makes the final crowns or bridge from the scan; try-in.
  • Days 4–5: final fitting, bite adjustment, polish.
  • Day 6–7 (full arch): second adjustment visit and final check before flying.

One honest warning: Trip 2 is where a failed implant is discovered. If one implant has not integrated, it is removed and replaced — and that implant needs its own healing period, which can mean a third, shorter trip months later. It is not common, but a plan with no room for it is a fragile plan. This is also why the warranty question matters before you book, not after.

The treatments that really are one trip

Not everything abroad needs two journeys. Work that sits on your own teeth, rather than in the bone, is finished in a single visit because there is nothing to integrate:

TreatmentTripsDays needed
Veneers15–7 days (some clinics quote 4–6)
Crowns15–7 days (some clinics quote 3–5)
Smile makeover without implants15–7 days
Whitening, bonding11–2 days

The pattern inside the week is the same: preparation and temporaries at the start, lab days in the middle, fitting at the end. If your plan mixes implants and veneers or crowns, the cosmetic work is usually done on Trip 2, once the implants have healed — so a mixed plan is still two trips in total.

Days off work: the honest count

Clinic pages count days in the chair. Employers count days away. For a UK or European patient flying to Turkey, a realistic count for a full-arch case is:

A single implant is lighter — roughly 3–4 working days across both trips — but it is still two separate blocks of leave, months apart, which is the part to agree with your employer early. Weekend flying can shave a day off each trip; it cannot merge the trips.

The real total: about £2,430 treatment + £1,125 travel = roughly £3,555, before any grafts or extractions, and before time off work. Two lessons from the arithmetic. First, the second trip's travel — over £500 here — is a real cost of implant treatment abroad, not an afterthought; any comparison that prices only one trip understates the total by roughly that much. Second, because travel is a fixed cost per trip, it weighs heaviest on small treatments: for a single implant, two trips of travel can approach the treatment saving, which is why single implants abroad make most sense combined with other work or a trip you wanted anyway.

FAQ

How many trips do I need for dental implants in Turkey?
Two. Trip 1 (typically 3–7 days) places the implants and fits temporary teeth; after 3–6 months of healing at home, Trip 2 (typically 3–7 days) fits the final crowns or bridge. Veneers and crowns, by contrast, need one trip of 5–7 days.

How long does full mouth implant treatment abroad take, start to finish?
Usually 4–7 months from first flight to final teeth: a 5–7 day first trip, a 3–6 month healing gap, and a 5–7 day second trip. The elapsed time is mostly healing, not treatment — you are at home and working for almost all of it.

Can I get dental implants in one trip?
You can get the first stage in one trip, and with immediate loading you can leave with fixed temporary teeth. The final teeth still require a second trip after the bone has fused to the implants (3–6 months). A plan that fits permanent teeth and sends you home finished in a week is compressing the healing stage, which is where early implant failures come from.

How many days do I need in Turkey for veneers?
One trip of 5–7 days is the standard plan (a few clinics quote 4–6): preparation and temporaries first, lab days in the middle, final bonding and checks at the end. No return trip is needed unless something goes wrong.

What happens if an implant has not healed by Trip 2?
The clinic will not load it. A failed implant is removed and, if the site allows, replaced — and the replacement needs its own healing period, which can mean a third trip. Ask before you book how the clinic handles this case and what its warranty covers; travel for extra trips is almost never covered.

When is the second trip booked?
Usually provisionally at Trip 1 and confirmed after a healing check (photos or an X-ray) near the end of the gap. Keep some flexibility in the dates until the clinic confirms integration — booking the cheapest non-refundable flight for a fixed day one week after surgery is how patients end up paying change fees.
